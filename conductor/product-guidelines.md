# Product Guidelines

## Tone and Voice
- **Friendly & Encouraging:** The interface should use simple, jargon-free language that is supportive and accessible. While maintaining professional standards, the voice should help non-professionals feel confident navigating a complex task.

## Visual Identity
- **Modern & Technical:** The UI will use clean lines, high-contrast elements, and a professional color palette (e.g., "Blueprint Blue" or "Architectural White"). Icons should be precise and reflect standard architectural symbols where appropriate, reinforcing the promise of professional results.

## Information Architecture & UX
- **Progressive Disclosure:** To avoid overwhelming the user, the application will start with simple, core tools (drawing walls, placing windows). Advanced options, such as detailed material metadata or structural flags, will be revealed only when necessary or requested by the user.

## Editor Interaction
- **Precision-First:** The drawing experience will prioritize accuracy. This includes strict grid snapping, support for explicit numeric input for wall lengths and angles, and clear measurement overlays. This ensures that area calculations and permit drawings are deterministic and accurate.

## Error Handling & Validation
- **Constructive & Actionable:** Validation feedback should be educational. Errors and warnings must clearly explain the issue and provide specific steps to resolve it (e.g., "Walls cannot overlap. Move the wall 5cm to the left to resolve"), helping the user learn architectural constraints as they design.
